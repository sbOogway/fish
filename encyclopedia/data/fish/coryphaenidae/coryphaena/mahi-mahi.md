## Description
The mahi-mahi, common dolphinfish, dolphinfish, dolphin or  dorado is a surface-dwelling ray-finned fish found in off-shore temperate, tropical, and subtropical waters worldwide. It is also widely called dorado and dolphin. It is one of two members of the family Coryphaenidae, the other being the pompano dolphinfish. These fish are most commonly found in the waters around the Gulf of Mexico, Costa Rica, Hawaii, and the Indian Ocean. In Italy it is called corifena, lampuga or pesce capone, and has even given its name to the caponata though eggplant has now taken the place of the fish.

## Biology
In East African waters, spawning season may last from March to early June and spawning occurs inshore. In the western Pacific, sex ratios were about equal during spawning season. Spawning in the western Atlantic occurs over an extended period of time. Spawn naturally in captivity without artificial inducement .
Spawning occurs mainly in Feb, Mar, Apr, May, Jun, Jul, Aug, Sep, Oct, Nov, Dec. Fecundity ranges from 58,000-1,500,000 eggs.
Its diet consists mainly of Amphipods, Benthic algae/weeds, Bony fish, Crabs, Gastropods, Isopods, Jellyfish/hydroids, Mysids, Octopi, Shrimps & prawns, Squids/cuttlefish, Stomatopods and turtles.
It may be targeted by predators such as Anous stolidus, Carcharhinus tilstoni, Coryphaena hippurus, Istiophorus albicans, Istiophorus platypterus, Makaira indica, Makaira mazara, Makaira nigricans, Sphyrna zygaena, Sterna fuscata, Tetrapturus albidus, Tetrapturus audax, Thunnus alalunga, Thunnus albacares, Xiphias gladius and unidentified whales.
