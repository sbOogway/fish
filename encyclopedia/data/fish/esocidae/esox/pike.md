The northern pike is a powerful, voracious freshwater predator found throughout the northern hemisphere. Its long, cylindrical body, flattened duck-bill snout, and mouth full of sharp teeth make it instantly recognisable — and among anglers it is one of the most sought-after game fish, prized for its explosive takes and spectacular fights.

## Physical Characteristics

Pike have a long, streamlined, cylindrical body with the dorsal and anal fins set far back near the tail, giving them sudden bursts of acceleration. The head is broad and flattened with a distinctive duck-bill snout and a large mouth filled with sharp, backward-pointing teeth.

The body colouring features pale, bean-shaped spots on a green to grey background, fading to a yellow-white belly. This camo pattern hides them superbly among weed beds. Typical adults run 50–90 cm, but specimens over 120 cm and 20 kg are the stuff of angling legend.

## Habitat & Distribution

Pike are found right across the northern hemisphere — throughout Europe, northern Asia, and North America. They favour slow-flowing rivers, lakes, and canals with abundant weed, reeds, and submerged structure where they can ambush prey.

They are most comfortable in well-vegetated water and prefer moderate temperatures around 10–20°C. In summer they retreat to deeper, cooler water or the shade of dense weed.

## Behavior

The pike is an ambush predator: it lies motionless in weed or near structure, then launches a short, explosive strike at passing prey. Its diet is broad — fish, frogs, and even ducklings and small mammals. Feeding occurs mainly at dawn and dusk, though pike can be caught through the day, especially in cooler conditions.

Pike are solitary, fiercely territorial fish. The largest specimens are old, slow-growing, and highly wary.

## Spawning

Pike spawn very early in the year (February–May) in shallow, flooded, weedy shallows where the water has just warmed to 4–10°C, often before the ice has fully gone. Eggs are scattered over submerged vegetation and hatched fry are self-sufficient from hatching, feeding first on zooplankton.

## Fishing for Pike

Pike are a premier predator-fishing target. They are caught using spinners, spoons, crankbaits, jerkbaits, and trolled lures, as well as deadbaits and livebaits fished on the bottom or under a float. Their take can be explosive, and once hooked they fight with powerful, lunging runs and spectacular leaps.

A wire trace is essential — their sharp teeth will cut through ordinary line instantly. Handling must be careful; use a landing net and unhook them with proper pike tools to protect both the fish and the angler.

**Best periods:** March–November, with peaks soon after spawning and again in autumn. Cold-water fishing can also be productive.

**Difficulty:** Intermediate — locating fish and striking at the right moment is the challenge, not the fight itself.
