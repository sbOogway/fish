## Description
The albacore, known also as the albicore or longfin tuna, is a species of tuna of the order Scombriformes. It lives in temperate and tropical waters across the globe in the epipelagic and mesopelagic zones. There are six distinct stocks known globally in the Atlantic, Pacific, and Indian oceans, as well as in the Mediterranean Sea. The albacore has an elongate, fusiform body with a conical snout, large eyes, and remarkably long pectoral fins. Its body is a deep blue dorsally and shades of silvery white ventrally. Individuals can reach up to 1.4 m in length.

## Biology
The sex ratio in catches is about 1:1 for immature individuals, but males predominate among mature fishes, which is possibly due to both differential mortality of sexes and differential growth rate after maturity.
Spawning occurs mainly in Jan, Feb, Mar, May, Jun, Nov, Dec. Fecundity ranges from 260,000-3,000,000 eggs.
Its diet consists mainly of Amphipods, Bony fish, Euphausiids, Jellyfish/hydroids, Lobsters and squids/cuttlefish.
It may be targeted by predators such as Thunnus albacares, Thunnus maccoyii, Thunnus obesus, billfishes and unidentified shark.
