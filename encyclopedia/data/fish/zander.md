# Zander (*Sander lucioperca*)

The zander is a freshwater predatory fish of the perch family, prized by anglers across Europe for its fighting ability and the challenge it presents. Often called "pike-perch" due to its perch-like body and pike-like teeth, it is one of the most popular coarse fishing targets in continental Europe.

## Physical Characteristics

Zander have an elongated, perch-like body with a pointed snout and two distinct dorsal fins — the first spiny, the second soft-rayed. Their most striking feature is their large, glassy eyes, adapted for low-light hunting. Adults typically reach 50–70 cm, with trophy specimens exceeding 100 cm and 10 kg.

Young zander display dark vertical bars on their flanks (similar to perch), which fade as the fish matures. The back is grey-green to olive, fading to silver-white on the belly.

## Habitat & Distribution

Zander are found across Europe and western/central Asia. They were introduced to the British Isles in the 19th century and have since spread through many river systems and lakes. They thrive in large, slow-moving rivers, reservoirs, and lakes with sandy or muddy substrates.

They prefer moderate depths (5–15 m) and tolerate murky water better than most percids, thanks to their enhanced low-light vision. Water temperature range is broad (2–28°C), with optimal activity at 18–23°C.

## Behavior

Zander are **ambush predators**, typically lying in wait near submerged structure (weed beds, fallen trees, drop-offs) and striking with a short, powerful burst. They feed primarily at dawn, dusk, and through the night — their large eyes give them a significant advantage in low light.

Their diet consists mainly of small fish (roach, rudd, bleak, perch fry), shifting to crustaceans and invertebrates as juveniles. They are solitary or found in small groups, rarely schooling.

## Spawning

Spawning occurs in spring (March–May) when water temperatures reach 8–12°C. Males build nests on hard substrates (gravel, stones, roots) and guard the eggs fiercely. A single female can release 100,000–300,000 eggs.

## Fishing for Zander

Zander are targeted using spinning, ledgering, float fishing, and trolling. They fight hard with determined headshakes and can be finicky biters, making them a rewarding challenge.

**Best periods:** April–November, with peaks during pre-spawn (March–April) and autumn (September–November). Night fishing is particularly effective.

**Difficulty:** Intermediate — requires patience, good water-reading skills, and sensitivity to detect subtle bites.

## Regulations

Fishing regulations for zander vary widely by country and region. Some areas require specific permits; others enforce seasonal closures during spawning. Always check local rules before fishing.
