# Spinning (Lure Fishing)

Spinning is one of the most versatile and widely practiced fishing techniques. An artificial lure is cast and retrieved to mimic live baitfish, triggering predatory strikes. It works in both fresh and salt water, for species ranging from small perch to massive tuna.

## Core Equipment

The basic spinning setup consists of a spinning rod, spinning reel, line, and lures. Rod length and power vary by target species — ultra-light setups for small freshwater predators, heavy-duty rigs for saltwater giants.

## Casting

The fundamental cast: open the bail, hold the line with your index finger, rotate your torso toward the target, and release the line as the rod passes vertical. Close the bail and begin your retrieve.

## Retrieval Techniques

How you retrieve the lure is as important as the lure itself:

- **Steady**: Constant speed — good for spinners and spoons
- **Stop-and-go**: Retrieve with pauses — triggers reaction strikes
- **Jigging**: Lift and drop — effective for vertical presentations
- **Twitching**: Short, sharp rod movements — mimics injured baitfish
- **Slow rolling**: Very slow steady retrieve — for cold water or pressured fish

## Reading Water

Success in spinning depends heavily on identifying where fish are holding:

- Submerged structure (weed, rocks, timber)
- Depth changes and drop-offs
- Current seams and slack water pockets
- Low-light periods (dawn, dusk, night)
