Distribution

Greenland Halibut inhabits Saltwater waters. Map shows fishbase occurrence records.

Description

The Greenland halibut or Greenland turbot belongs to the family Pleuronectidae, and is the only species of the genus Reinhardtius. It is a predatory fish that mostly ranges at depths between 500 and 1,000 m (1,600–3,300 ft), and is found in the cold northern Atlantic, northern Pacific, and Arctic Oceans.

Size

Typical length is 56 cm, with a maximum recorded length of 110 cm.

Weight

Maximum recorded weight is 7.0 kg.

Age

Lifespan is around 36 years.

Sexual maturity is reached at about 9.5 years of age.

Biology

Fertilized eggs develop in approximately 50 days. Larvae are pelagic with a maximum size of 8 cm and will drift for about 4 months before settling at about 7 cm on clay-silt subtrates at depths of 300-450 m, after which they metamorphose . Spawning occurs mainly in Jan, Feb, Mar, Apr, May, Jun, Nov, Dec. Fecundity ranges from 6,800-158,000 eggs. Its diet consists mainly of Amphipods, Bony fish, Copepods, Crabs, Euphausiids, Gastropods, Hard corals, Mysids, Octopi, Polychaetes, Sea stars/brittle stars, Shrimps & prawns and squids/cuttlefish. It may be targeted by predators such as Amblyraja radiata, Cystophora cristata, Orcinus orca, Raja radiata, Reinhardtius hippoglossoides, Somniosus microcephalus and Theragra chalcogramma.

Behavior

Forms schools (sometimes, as juveniles and adults).

Forms loose shoals (sometimes, as juveniles and adults).

Seasonality

Its most productive fishing months in the Northern Hemisphere are typically May, Jun, Jul, Aug, Sep, Oct, though exact timing depends on local climate and water temperature.

Conservation

IUCN conservation status: LC.
