Distribution

Red Porgy inhabits Saltwater waters. Records span a broad geographic range. Map shows fishbase occurrence records.

Description

The red porgy, also known as the common seabream or Couch's bream, is a species of marine ray-finned fish in the family Sparidae. It is found in shallow waters on either side of the Atlantic Ocean, being present on the western coast of Europe and the Mediterranean Sea as well as the eastern coasts of North and South America and the Caribbean Sea. It feeds on or near the seabed and most individuals start life as females and later change sex to males.

Size

Typical length is 35 cm, with a maximum recorded length of 91 cm.

Weight

Maximum recorded weight is 7.7 kg.

Age

Lifespan is around 26 years.

Sexual maturity is reached at about 2 years of age.

Biology

Protogynous hermaphrodite species. First sexual maturity occurs at 3 years of age (about 24 cm), first behaving as females and then as males. Spawning happens at 1 5-19° and it tends to be in spring, depending on the habitat (in the Canary Islands it can start in January). Also Ref. 28504, 41163. Spawning occurs mainly in Apr, May, Jun, Jul, Aug. Fecundity ranges from 48,660-488,600 eggs. Its diet consists mainly of Amphipods, Bony fish, Crabs, Polychaetes, Shrimps & prawns and squids/cuttlefish. It may be targeted by predators such as Delphinus delphis, Galeorhinus galeus and Seriola dumerili.

Seasonality

Its most productive fishing months in the Northern Hemisphere are typically Apr, May, Jun, Sep, Oct, though exact timing depends on local climate and water temperature.

Conservation

IUCN conservation status: LC.
