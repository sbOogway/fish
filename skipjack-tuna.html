Distribution

Skipjack Tuna inhabits Saltwater waters. Records span a broad geographic range. Map shows fishbase occurrence records.

Description

The skipjack tuna is a perciform fish in the tuna family, Scombridae, and is the only member of the genus Katsuwonus. It is a cosmopolitan pelagic fish found in tropical and warm-temperate waters. Skipjack tuna can grow up to 1.1 m in length, and is a very important species for fisheries.

Size

Typical length is 80 cm, with a maximum recorded length of 110 cm.

Weight

Maximum recorded weight is 34.5 kg.

Age

Lifespan is around 12 years.

Sexual maturity is reached at about 3 years of age.

Biology

In tropical waters, reproductively active female skipjack tuna spawn almost daily. Spawning occurs mainly in Jan, Feb, Mar, Apr, May, Jun, Jul, Aug, Sep, Oct, Nov, Dec. Fecundity ranges from 80,000-2,000,000 eggs. Its diet consists mainly of Amphipods, Bony fish, Euphausiids, Fish eggs & larvae, Lobsters, Octopi, Shrimps & prawns and squids/cuttlefish. It may be targeted by predators such as Acanthocybium solandri, Anous stolidus, Carcharhinus albimarginatus, Coryphaena hippurus, Isurus oxyrinchus, Katsuwonus pelamis, Makaira indica, Makaira mazara, Makaira nigricans, Sterna fuscata, Thunnus albacares, Thunnus obesus, billfishes and whaler sharks.

Seasonality

Its most productive fishing months in the Northern Hemisphere are typically Jan, Feb, Mar, Apr, May, Jun, Jul, Aug, Sep, Oct, Nov, Dec, though exact timing depends on local climate and water temperature.

Conservation

IUCN conservation status: LC.
