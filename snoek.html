Distribution

Description

Leionura atun, known as the snoek in South Africa and as the barracouta in Australasia, is a long, thin species of snake mackerel found in the seas of the Southern Hemisphere, and a common food fish in South Africa, particularly along the west and southwest coast. Despite its Australasian name, it is not closely related to the barracuda.

Biology

Spawning patterns are complex, with different stock spawning at different times of the year . Spawning occurs mainly in Jan, Feb, Mar, Apr, May, Jun, Jul, Aug, Sep, Oct, Nov, Dec. Its diet consists mainly of Bony fish, Euphausiids and squids/cuttlefish. It may be targeted by predators such as Arctocephalus forsteri, Arctocephalus pusillus pusillus, Galeorhinus galeus, Merluccis capensis, Merluccius paradoxus, Mustelus antarcticus, Nototodarus gouldi, Prionace glauca, Raja alba, Raja clavata, Raja wallacei, Thyrsites atun and Xiphias gladius.

Behavior

Form schools near the bottom or midwater; sometimes even at the surface at night . Commonly called as Baracoutta and because it is a fast predator, several English vessels were named after it.
