Distribution

Wahoo inhabits Saltwater waters. Records span a broad geographic range. Map shows fishbase occurrence records.

Description

The wahoo is a scombrid fish found worldwide in tropical and subtropical seas. In Hawaii, the wahoo is known as ono. The species is sometimes called hoo in the United States. It is best known to sports fishermen, as its speed and high quality makes it a prized and valued game fish.

Size

Typical length is 170 cm, with a maximum recorded length of 250 cm.

Weight

Maximum recorded weight is 83.0 kg.

Age

Sexual maturity is reached at about 2 years of age.

Biology

Spawning seems to extend over a long period; fish in different maturity stages are frequently caught at the same time. Spawning occurs mainly in May, Jun, Jul, Aug, Sep, Oct. Fecundity ranges from 6,000,000 eggs. Its diet consists mainly of Benthic algae/weeds, Bony fish and squids/cuttlefish. It may be targeted by predators such as Carcharhinus albimarginatus.

Behavior

Often solitary

Seasonality

Its most productive fishing months in the Northern Hemisphere are typically Jan, Feb, Mar, Apr, Sep, Oct, Nov, Dec, though exact timing depends on local climate and water temperature.

Conservation

IUCN conservation status: LC.
