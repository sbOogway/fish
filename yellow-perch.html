Distribution

Description

The yellow perch, commonly referred to as perch, striped perch, American perch or preacher is a freshwater ray-finned fish from the perch family Percidae, native to much of central North America, particularly the Great Lakes–St. Lawrence River basin and the Upper Mississippi River catchment. It was first described in 1814 by Samuel Latham Mitchill from New York, and is closely related and morphologically similar to the European perch, and is sometimes considered a subspecies of its European counterpart.

Biology

Nonobligatory plant spawner. Spawning occurs mainly in Mar, Apr, May. Fecundity ranges from 3,035-109,000 eggs. Its diet consists mainly of Amphipods, Bony fish, Cladocerans, Insects, Isopods and ostracods. It may be targeted by predators such as Acanthocyclops vernalis, Amia calva, Anguilla rostrata, Diacyclops thomasi, Esox lucius, Esox masquinongy, Lota lota, Micropterus dolomieu, Micropterus salmoides, Oncorhynchus kisutch, Oncorhynchus mykiss, Oncorhynchus tshawytscha, Perca flavescens, Salvelinus namaycush, Sander vitreus, Stizostedion canadense, Stizostedion vitreum, unidentified basses, unidentified crappies and unidentified sunfishes.
